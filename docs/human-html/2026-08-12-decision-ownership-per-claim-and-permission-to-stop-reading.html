Ownership per claim, and permission to stop reading

Kind decision Created 2026-08-12 Owner Rehan Fazal Status Draft, not yet reviewed Read time ~6 min Source brainstorm on an external AI writing policy

In plain terms

  • What this does for the reader: An artifact says who is accountable for each judgment it makes, and says when you can stop reading it.
  • Why it matters: In a live lane of 197 artifacts, the median length grew from 1,982 words in May to 4,359 in August, and every recent artifact carries a reading guide that does not stop the growth.
  • What's being asked: Approve the two calls below. Three related questions stay open and are routed.
  • What would change this: The claim that navigation is not the fix rests on one lane, written by one author. A second lane, different author, where readers demonstrably stop early would overturn it. Nothing else below changes the call.

Decision

In the context of a content contract that says what an artifact must contain and never who stands behind it, facing a live lane whose median artifact has roughly doubled in length while its validator sits red and unrun, we decided that ownership attaches to individual claims rather than to whole artifacts, and that every summary states what would change its own conclusion, to achieve an artifact a reader can act on and stop reading, accepting that both are judgment calls a validator can only nudge toward, never certify.

Rehan Fazal holds this call. The part least settled is the second half: the stopping rule is argued from one lane's evidence and one reader's experience of it.

Context

Clay publishes an internal AI writing policy for text that other people read. Six principles, and the two that bite here are that more time should go into authoring a document than into consuming it, and that writing is thinking, so a document generated from a short prompt is proof of nothing. The rest of it this skill already enforces, in some cases more strictly than the policy asks: the anti-slop checklist, the em dash rule, and the prose discipline in the spine all attack the padding the policy names.

Two of its principles have no counterpart here, and they turn out to be one gap seen twice. Nothing in the contract asks whether the artifact was earned, and nothing tells a reader they may stop.

The forcing function is measured rather than argued. A private lane of 197 artifacts, all standard mode, all one author, produced under successive versions of this skill:

The median artifact carries 10 h2 sections against a kind skeleton of about 5, and only 18 of 197 sit at or under their skeleton. That is the mechanism: required-section is a floor with no ceiling, so each artifact accretes on the last. A reading guide is present on every recent artifact and does not interrupt it, which is the finding that matters, because it rules out navigation as the fix. A table of contents over seventeen sections says where everything is and never says what may be skipped.

Two supporting observations from the same corpus. The reviewer field holds a human name 65 times, an agent name 29 times, an unfilled placeholder 10 times, and some spelling of pending 12 times across four different spellings, which is an author hand-rolling a review state the schema does not offer. And artifact-read-time does not track length at all: 194 words claims five minutes, 4,701 words claims five minutes, and the longest artifact gives up and says browse.

Current vs proposed

AspectCurrentProposed
Where ownership sits Nowhere defined. A name may appear in the ribbon, in reviewer, against an open question, or against a corrective action, meaning four different things. Per claim. A block that commits somebody carries data-owner and names the holder in prose, beside one sentence of their own doubt. The ribbon keeps artifact-wide accountability.
What reviewer asserts Undefined, and checked only for being non-empty, so a plausible name satisfies it. That a human read it before it was forwarded. Absent by default, and the footer says so rather than inventing a name.
What the summary closes Nothing. Three bullets orient the reader; none concludes, so the reader audits to the bottom to find out whether the top was complete. A fourth bullet naming what would change the conclusion and where that lives. Closure asserted, surprise located, permission to stop granted.
Which kinds this keys off Kind decides section shape, and the two kinds that carry both description and judgment fit neither column. No kind list. A heading regex finds the judgment section the way comparison-visual already finds a comparison, so kind sensitivity emerges instead of being declared.

The rows are not independent. Each one replaces an unconstrained field with a claim a reader can hold somebody to, which is the single move this decision makes four times.

Consequences

  • Positive: the two straddling kinds stop being anomalies, because ownership attaches below the level where they split. Dynamic mode inherits both rules with no special case, since neither depends on which sections exist. And most of the change is subtraction: the twelve shipped examples currently assert a reviewer who read nothing, and deleting a false claim costs the reader no space at all.
  • Neutral: a new data-owner marker joins the existing family, and the judgment heading check reuses the section-scoped mechanic already implemented for comparisons.
  • Negative: both calls are judgment. A marker check can catch the artifact that never tried and can never certify the one that did, which is the same ceiling every rule in the mechanical floor already has. The stopping rule also invites a formula: a fourth bullet written to satisfy a rule, saying nothing would change the conclusion, is worse than no bullet, because it claims a closure nobody tested.

Still open

Does required-section get a ceiling, and in what unit? Rehan Fazal 2026-09-01 Leaning prose words, not bytes. The existing size-budget rule counts 512 KiB of file, which inline SVG and CSS dominate, so it fired once in 197 artifacts and misses everything a reader feels. Without a decision the growth continues, since nothing else opposes it.
Should artifact-read-time be computed rather than declared? Rehan Fazal 2026-09-01 Leaning compute it. The script already parses the document, so new and index can fill it, or check can warn past a wide tolerance. Left alone the field stays decorative, and it understates by roughly half on the artifacts where a reader most needs the budget.
Does the review state get named values? Rehan Fazal 2026-09-01 Leaning three values, covering unreviewed, agent-reviewed and human-reviewed. An agent name in reviewer is honest reporting the schema has no word for. Left alone, the four spellings of pending keep multiplying.

Reversibility

Cheap in both directions. data-owner is an attribute other artifacts ignore, and the judgment heading check would ship as a warning, which is the staged rollout the skill already documents for a new rule. The summary bullet is prose, so withdrawing it costs one scaffold edit and leaves every existing artifact valid.

The expensive half is not the rules. It is that the contract has to be running for either to matter, and in the lane that produced the evidence above, check exits 1 today on fourteen blocking violations. Both calls here are worth nothing until that is green.
